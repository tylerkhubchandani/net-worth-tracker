Performance over time

Return uses the Modified Dietz method, which adjusts for when contributions were made. It covers accounts that have holdings and is approximate. It gets more accurate as you record more snapshots by updating prices over time.

Accounts with holdings calculate their balance automatically (shares x price), so you never enter that money twice. Updating a price below records a new snapshot on the date set in the Accounts tab. Prices are entered by hand for now.

Import holdings from CSV

Export your positions from your brokerage as a CSV file, then choose it here. The file is read in your browser and is never uploaded anywhere.

CSV stands for "comma-separated values." It's a plain spreadsheet file that most brokerages let you export, and you can open it in Excel or Google Sheets.

Projection

A hypothetical illustration, not a prediction or financial advice. It starts from your current net worth and scheduled monthly contributions, assumes contributions stay the same, and compounds returns monthly. Conservative and optimistic use your expected return minus and plus the range. Only goals that track net worth are drawn.

About this project

I'm Tyler Khubchandani, a first-year finance student at the Community College of Rhode Island. I built this tracker to learn how portfolio analytics and long-term projections work by building them firsthand.

What you can try

Add your accounts and holdings, or import a brokerage CSV. View allocation, concentration, and gain / loss by holding, account, or asset class. Set a goal and run conservative, base, and optimistic projections. Track contributions, including scheduled and recurring deposits.

How it works

Everything runs in your browser, and your data is saved only on this device. Prices are either entered by hand or imported from a CSV.

How it's built

A single-page web app in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, built with the help of an AI assistant.

Educational tool, not financial advice. Projections are hypothetical, and returns are not guaranteed.
